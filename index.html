Made with AI? A First Large-Scale Measurement of AI Disclosure Labels on YouTube

Muhammad Abubakar Mughal, Muhammad Muiz Farhan, Umar Habib, Ihsan Ayyub Qazi, Zafar Ayyub Qazi

Key finding 36% of trending videos carry an AI disclosure label. Made with AI videos get 19% more views and reach 16.5% more countries, yet receive 47.7% fewer comments.

Details
  • Dataset. Collected 5.2M+ trending entries (680K+ unique videos) across 111 countries over two periods (2025, 2026), with 270K+ recommended videos: the first large-scale dataset of YouTube's AI disclosure labels.
  • Method. Built a custom scraper using Selenium and ChromeDriver to extract Made with AI and Auto-dubbed labels from video pages, which the YouTube Data API does not expose.
  • Prevalence. 36% of trending videos carry an AI disclosure label; Auto-dubbed grew 4× between 2025 and 2026, and Made with AI is concentrated in low- and middle-income countries (8% vs. 1%).
  • Engagement. Made with AI videos get 19% more views and reach 16.5% more countries, yet 47.7% fewer comments, while recommendations alone do not amplify them (~5% of suggestions).
  • User study. In an IRB-approved study with 60 participants, low-literacy users identified only 42% of AI-generated videos correctly, against 77% for high-literacy users.

Read Once, Judge Many: Separating Placement from Audience in Ad Moderation on Children's Videos

Abid Ali, Muhammad Abubakar Mughal, Ihsan Ayyub Qazi, Zafar Ayyub Qazi, Usman Naseem

Key finding Models can tell whether an ad is made for children (PR-AUC 0.95) but not whether it should be kept off children's videos (0.41). Commercial models score near chance on the second question; supervision raises it to 0.91.

Details
  • Problem. Existing ad labels mix two questions: should an ad be kept off videos made for children (the placement rule), and is the ad made for children (the audience rule)? They can diverge: a money-transfer app ad is not made for children, yet may run beside their videos. Separating the two shows where moderation fails.
  • Method. Read Once, Judge Many extracts each ad's evidence once (up to 16 keyframes, a Whisper transcript, on-screen text, sound-event tags and metadata), reads it as a shared prefix in Qwen3-VL-8B, and reuses its KV cache to answer every rule as a single yes/no token in one batched pass.
  • Efficiency. Each added rule costs about 1% of a full forward pass; at 16 rules, batched scoring is 12.3× faster than a separate pass per rule (model execution only, excluding evidence extraction).
  • Data. 2,303 ads served on YouTube Made for Kids channels across 30 countries (KidsAds), with transfer tested on 1,287 brand-safety videos from TikTok, YouTube and Meta (Zefr) and 1,395 malicious-cartoon clips (MOB).
  • Commercial models. Claude Sonnet 5.5, GPT-6.1 Sol and Gemini 3.8 Flash score 0.22–0.30 on the placement rule, near the 0.26 positive rate. They answer the audience rule well, then treat "made for children" as a reason not to block. The platform's published policy text raises them to 0.57–0.60; model tier and more reasoning move them by at most 0.10.
  • Supervision. Policy text with 64 labelled examples reaches 0.74, a linear probe on frozen features 0.79, and LoRA fine-tuning 0.91, flagging 92.2% of inappropriate ads at the zero-shot model's false-positive budget (vs. 28.9%).
  • Limits. On brand-safety videos and malicious cartoons, the gain over zero-shot is not significant; on cartoons, the model cannot perceive the cues that decide the label.

Cultural Personas in LLM-Based Ad Moderation for Children's Content

Ongoing
  • Investigating whether YouTube's universal ad policy for Made for Kids content is applied rigidly or flexibly when LLM moderators are given different identities (country, gender, age, political affiliation).
  • Built a moderation pipeline in which Gemini 3.5 Flash-Lite watches 300 human-annotated ads under 13 conditions (a baseline and 12 country personas spanning all Child Online Safety Index quartiles), totalling 4,200 requests.
  • Preliminary results show that country personas change labels on 10–15% of ads against 6% run-to-run noise, yet the changes follow no cultural pattern and no rationale mentions the persona's country.
  • Extending the pipeline with ad metadata and Whisper audio transcripts, and replicating it on OpenAI models (GPT-6.1 Terra, GPT-6 Luna) through the Batch API.
