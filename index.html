The Synthetic Stream: Prevalence and Engagement of AI Content on YouTube

Muhammad Muiz Farhan*, Muhammad Abubakar Mughal*, Ihsan Ayyub Qazi, Zafar Ayyub Qazi

* Equal contribution

Key finding AI-tagged videos gained 10.2% more views but 46.7% fewer likes, suggesting passive consumption without active endorsement.

Details
  • Problem. Platforms claim to label AI content, but we lack empirical data on how prevalent this content is and how users actually engage with it.
  • Method. Designed a custom scraping pipeline using Selenium and the YouTube Data API v3 to collect a global dataset of 1.13M+ trending videos and 224k recommendations.
  • Findings.
    • Prevalence: AI-tagged videos constitute ~11% of the global YouTube trending feed.
    • Temporal trends: regression analysis revealed a growth of ~2.2% in AI-tagged videos over a 36-day collection span.
    • Engagement paradox: 10.2% more views but 46.7% fewer likes.
    • Algorithmic traps: viewing just one AI video raised the probability of subsequent AI exposure by 51%.
    • User study: low digitally literate users detected AI-generated content with only 42% accuracy, compared to 77% for high digitally literate users; most low-literacy users did not report checking video descriptions for disclosure labels.

Security Evaluation of Agentic Browsers under Injection-Based Attacks

Oct 2025 – Present

Distributed & AI Systems Lab, LUMS

  • Context. As AI agents (like Perplexity Comet and ChatGPT Atlas) begin browsing the web autonomously, they face increasingly complex attacks that standard LLMs do not.
  • Method. Designing an experimental framework to evaluate browser agents against prompt-injection and jailbreak attacks across major social media platforms.
  • Goal. To expose vulnerabilities where malicious text in the DOM can trick an agent into overriding its safety guardrails.
